# LLM Wiki CLI — DOX Concept (Detailed)

This document explains how the LLM Wiki CLI uses the **DOX Framework** as inspiration for organizing the wiki. It is written for anyone joining the project without prior context, so they understand the philosophy, the contract hierarchy, and the difference between the DOX original and this project's adaptation.

---

## 1. What is the DOX Framework?

The DOX Framework is a system for organizing documentation in a cascading, hierarchical way. Its central idea is that a large knowledge base should be broken into smaller, self-contained units, each governed by a binding contract that explains what belongs there and how to navigate it.

The canonical DOX reference is:

> https://github.com/agent0ai/dox/blob/main/AGENTS.md

In the original DOX design, the top-level document is `AGENTS.md`. It acts as the constitution of the knowledge base: it defines the folder structure, page types, naming conventions, tag taxonomy, and rules for the entire system. Sub-folders may have their own more specific contracts, creating a cascade of documentation from the general to the specific.

The key insights from DOX are:

- **Cascading contracts:** The contract at the top level governs the whole; contracts at lower levels govern their own sub-trees.
- **Self-describing folders:** Every folder has a contract that explains what it contains, so a reader can navigate without guessing.
- **Binding rules:** The contract is not just advice; it is the specification that the system (and any AI agent) follows.
- **Separation of structure from content:** The contract defines the structure; the pages are the content.

This project adapts these ideas, but changes the mechanism: the contracts are written in `index.md` files, not `AGENTS.md`.

---

## 2. Why `index.md` Instead of `AGENTS.md`?

In the original DOX Framework, `AGENTS.md` is both the human-readable contract and the machine-readable specification. In the LLM Wiki CLI, these two roles are split:

| Role | File | Audience | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contract** | `index.md` hierarchy | Humans and research agents | Navigate the wiki; understand what each folder contains and how pages are linked. |
| **Ingestion guide** | `AGENTS.md` | The LLM orchestrator | Tell the LLM how to organize and write this specific wiki. |

The reason for this split is practical. The wiki is generated by the LLM, and the generated structure is dynamic: it depends on what the corpus actually contains. The `index.md` files are the result of the generation process; they describe the wiki as it exists. The `AGENTS.md` file is the input to the generation process; it tells the LLM what to aim for.

If `AGENTS.md` were both the input and the contract, the human reader would have to read the LLM's instructions to understand the wiki. By separating them, the human reader can navigate the clean `index.md` hierarchy, while the LLM can read the detailed `AGENTS.md` instructions.

## 3. The Contract Hierarchy

The contract hierarchy is a cascade of `index.md` files. Each level is a child of the level above it, and each level describes and governs the levels below it.

```
workspace/
├── index-of-indexes.md          # Level 0: the root roadmap
└── wikis/
    └── <slug>/
        ├── index.md             # Level 1: the wiki-level contract
        └── <folder>/
            ├── index.md         # Level 2: the folder-level contract
            └── <subfolder>/     # Optional deeper level for typed entity groups
                ├── index.md     # Level 3: the sub-folder contract
                └── <page>.md   # Level 4: content pages
```

For most folders, the hierarchy stops at Level 2. Typed entity groups are an exception: the `entities/` folder may contain sub-folders such as `people/`, `organizations/`, or corpus-specific groups like `regulators/`, each with its own `index.md` child contract.

### 3.1 Level 0: `index-of-indexes.md`

The root contract. It is the entry point for the entire workspace. It lists:

- Every wiki in the workspace.
- The number of sources and pages in each wiki.
- The scope or purpose of each wiki.
- Cross-wiki names and links, if any.

Its job is to answer the question: *What wikis exist in this workspace, and where should I start?*

### 3.2 Level 1: Wiki-Level `index.md`

The contract for a single wiki. It lists:

- The wiki's title and scope.
- The dynamic folders inside the wiki.
- The page types used in the wiki.
- Navigation guidance (e.g., "start with documents for raw text, then entities for people and companies").
- Citation conventions and naming rules.
- Statistics: sources, document pages, entity pages, topic pages, raw pages.

Its job is to answer the question: *What is this wiki about, and how is it organized?*

### 3.3 Level 2: Folder-Level `index.md`

The contract for a dynamic folder. It is the most specific contract for most folders, and it is the one most inspired by the DOX sub-folder contracts. It lists:

- The folder's purpose.
- What page types live in the folder.
- The naming convention for files in the folder.
- How pages in this folder are linked to other folders.
- Any folder-specific rules (e.g., "every table in this folder must preserve its original header row").
- A catalog of the pages in the folder.

Its job is to answer the question: *What kind of pages are in this folder, and how do I read them?*

### 3.4 Level 3: Sub-Folder-Level `index.md` (Typed Entity Groups)

The `entities/` folder is typically split into typed sub-folders, such as `entities/people/`, `entities/organizations/`, or corpus-specific groups like `entities/regulators/`. Each sub-folder has its own `index.md` child contract that describes:

- The sub-folder's purpose (e.g., "individuals mentioned in the corpus").
- The entity types placed here.
- The naming convention for entity pages in this sub-folder.
- How entity pages link to related documents, topics, and other entity pages.

These sub-folder contracts are generated during the `sample` phase and updated during `ingest` as the entity taxonomy evolves.

### 3.5 Level 4: Content Pages

The actual wiki articles. Each page has a `type` in its frontmatter that ties it to the folder-level or sub-folder-level contract. The page is both readable content and a node in the contract graph: it links to other pages, and other pages link to it.

## 4. Dynamic Folders and the Sample Phase

Unlike the original DOX Framework, where the folder structure is defined up-front in `AGENTS.md`, the LLM Wiki CLI discovers the folder structure during the `sample` phase.

The process is:

1. The user defines the wiki's purpose in `AGENTS.md` during `init`.
2. The user places PDFs in `raw/`.
3. The LLM analyzes the corpus during `sample` and proposes a folder structure that fits the data.
4. The LLM writes the folder-level `index.md` contracts.
5. During `ingest`, the LLM may discover that the structure needs to change. It can autonomously create new folders or page types and record the change in the structural change log.

This dynamic structure is essential because the corpus is unknown at the start. The LLM cannot know in advance whether the Panama Papers are best organized by jurisdiction, by company, by person, or by document type until it has read enough of the corpus. The DOX idea of cascading contracts is preserved, but the contracts are generated by the LLM rather than written by hand.

---

## 5. The Contract as a Binding Document

Each `index.md` is a binding contract. When a page is created, it must be consistent with the contracts above it. This means:

- A page must be placed in a folder that accepts its `type`.
- A page must follow the naming convention of its folder.
- A page must use the citation convention defined in the wiki-level `index.md`.
- A page must link to other pages in a way consistent with the folder-level contract.

If the LLM wants to break a contract, it applies a structural change autonomously. For example, if the folder-level contract says `donors/` contains entity pages, and the LLM wants to create a `donations/` folder with `transaction` pages, it can do so and record the change. The LLM may either add the new page type to an existing folder or create a new folder, depending on which better fits the corpus. There is no human approval gate, but the change is logged so the human can review it after the fact.

---

## 6. Updating the Contracts

Contracts are not static. They are updated whenever the wiki changes:

- After `sample`: the wiki-level and folder-level contracts are written for the first time.
- After `ingest`: the folder-level contracts are updated to include new pages, new page types, and new statistics.
- After a structural change: the affected contracts are rewritten to reflect the new structure.
- After re-ingestion: the contracts are updated to reflect any new or removed pages.

Because the contracts are generated, they never drift far from the actual content of the wiki. They are always a faithful map of what exists.

---

## 7. How DOX Helps the Research Agent

The cascading contracts are designed to help a downstream research agent (or a human journalist) navigate the wiki efficiently. The agent does not need to read every page to know what the wiki contains. It can:

- Read `index-of-indexes.md` to see which wikis exist.
- Read the wiki-level `index.md` to see which folders and page types exist.
- Read a folder-level `index.md` to see which pages of a particular type exist.
- Follow wikilinks from a page to related pages.

This is the DOX idea of **deterministic navigation**: the agent can traverse the knowledge base through defined contracts and links, rather than searching blindly through raw documents.

---

## 8. Relationship to `AGENTS.md`

It is important to keep the roles separate:

- `AGENTS.md` is the **ingestion guide**. It tells the LLM how to create the wiki. It may contain detailed instructions, examples, and conventions. It is written for the LLM.
- `index.md` is the **contract**. It documents the wiki as it exists. It is written for humans and research agents.

For example, `AGENTS.md` might say:

> "For political donation filings, create a `donors/` folder for donor entities and a `recipients/` folder for politicians and parties. Every donation amount must be cited."

The resulting folder-level `index.md` for `donors/` would say:

> "This folder contains entity pages for individuals and organizations that made political donations. Each page links to the donation pages where the donor appears. File names are normalized lowercase slugs."

Both are hierarchical and cascading, but they serve different readers and purposes.

---

## 9. Summary of DOX-Inspired Rules

The project takes the following ideas from DOX:

1. **Cascading contracts:** Every folder has a contract; every contract is a child of a higher-level contract.
2. **Self-describing structure:** A reader can understand the wiki by reading the `index.md` files, without opening every content page.
3. **Binding rules:** The contracts define what belongs where and how pages are linked.
4. **Dynamic discovery:** The contracts are generated by the LLM based on the corpus, not hand-written in advance.

And it changes the following from DOX:

1. **Contract file:** Contracts are in `index.md`, not `AGENTS.md`.
2. **Role of `AGENTS.md`:** `AGENTS.md` is the LLM ingestion guide, not the human-readable contract.
3. **Folder creation:** Folders are discovered during `sample` and `ingest`, not fixed in the initial contract.

If the contracts are kept accurate and the content pages follow them, the wiki becomes a navigable, verifiable, and maintainable knowledge base that a journalist or research agent can use to find connections without re-reading the original documents.

Typed entity groups add one extra level to the cascade: `entities/<subfolder>/index.md` contracts sit between the `entities/` folder contract and the individual entity pages.
